In short

Yulovo keeps your memories on your phone. If you sign in, they are also stored in your account, so they are safe when you change phones and you can share them with the friends you add.

We do not show ads, do not track you across other apps or websites, and do not sell or rent your data to anyone.

This policy explains what the app collects, why, who else handles it, how long it is kept and how you control it.

Who is responsible

Yulovo is published by Timur Egamov, an individual developer (“we”, “us”). We decide how your personal data is processed and are responsible for it under Vietnam’s personal data protection law, including Decree 13/2023/ND-CP. Contact: timuregamov2000@gmail.com.

On your phone

Your memories (photos, title, story, dates, place name, address and coordinates, the people and tags you add), your trail and your settings are stored in a database on your phone.

If you do not sign in, all of this stays on your phone only. We receive none of it. The place-name and map lookups described below still go to those services.

The optional Face ID lock is handled by iOS. The app only learns whether unlocking succeeded and never receives biometric data.

Your account

You can sign in with Apple or with Google. We receive an account identifier from Apple or Google and your email address. If you choose to hide your email with Apple, we receive a private relay address from Apple instead. Apple may also pass your name the first time you sign in. It becomes your display name, which you can change at any time.

Your account is kept by our backend provider, Supabase (Supabase Auth). We never see or store a password.

Sync and storage

When you are signed in, your memories, photos and trail are synced to our database and private file storage, run by Supabase in Singapore (region ap-southeast-1). Memories you made before signing in move into your account.

Access rules on the server let only you read your own data. Friends see only what is described in the next section. All connections are encrypted (HTTPS).

What friends see

You add friends with an invite code or link. Anyone who has your code or link can see your display name before adding you. You can make a new code at any time; the old one then stops working.

Your friends see your display name and profile picture, and every memory of yours that is not set to “Only me”: its title, photos, place name and exact pin, dates, the friends you tagged, and the hearts and comments on it with the names and pictures of the people who left them.

Friends never see your stories, the people and tags you type into a memory, or memories set to “Only me”.

Your trail (where you have been, the uncovered fog) is not shared unless you turn on “Show friends where I have been” in Friends. Friends then see the uncovered areas, rounded to about 100 m. This is off for everyone until they turn it on, including accounts made before this version.

Friends can heart and comment on your memories, tag you in theirs and add photos to memories they tagged you in. A memory you are tagged in appears on your map. Your hearts, comments and the photos you add can be seen, with your name and picture, by everyone who can see that memory, including the owner’s other friends.

Blocking and reports

When you block someone, the block is stored on our server so it can be enforced.

When you report a memory, comment, photo or person, we receive who sent the report, who and what it is about, the reason, any note you add and a copy of what was reported (such as the comment’s text or the memory’s title), so we can still review it if the author deletes it. We use reports only to review them and deal with abuse.

Notifications

Push notifications are sent only if you allow them. We then store your device’s Expo push token, the app’s language and the platform, linked to your account.

Notifications about friends’ activity (a new memory, a heart, a comment, a tag, added photos) pass through Expo’s push service (USA) and the Apple Push Notification service. They contain the friend’s name, the memory’s title and, for a comment, up to its first 80 characters.

“On this day” reminders are scheduled on your phone only. Their content never leaves it.

Camera and photos

Photos for memories are taken with the camera inside the app. Each photo is saved again as a new JPEG, which removes all of its EXIF metadata, including GPS. The memory’s location is kept as a separate field instead.

Your photo library opens only when you choose a profile picture or add photos to a friend’s memory. The app reads only the photos you pick, and saves those again in the same way.

A share card is a newly drawn image with no location in it. It is saved to your Photos only when you ask.

Location

When you take a photo, the app reads your location once to pin the memory where you are. This needs location access while you use the app.

With Explore mode on, the app also records your location in the background, about one point for every 50 m you move, to uncover the fog on your map. iOS shows the location indicator while this happens. You can turn Explore mode off in Settings, or change location access in iOS Settings, at any time.

Under Vietnamese law, location data is sensitive personal data. We process it only with your consent, which you give by allowing location access and turning on Explore mode, and only for these features.

Other services

Place names: to name the place of a memory, its coordinates are sent to Photon by komoot (Germany), which returns a name and address. No account details are sent, but the service sees your IP address. Without a connection, the memory simply has no place name.

Map: map tiles come from OpenFreeMap and are built from OpenStreetMap data. The tile servers see your IP address and the map area you load, like any website.

App updates: Expo (EAS Update, USA) delivers updates to the app. It sees your device’s IP address, the app version and the platform.

Opening in Maps: if you open a place in Apple Maps or Google Maps, its coordinates are passed to that app, which has its own privacy policy. Signing in with Apple or Google is also covered by their own policies.

Yulovo Plus

Yulovo Plus is bought through the App Store. Apple handles the payment and knows your Apple ID; we never see your card. RevenueCat (USA), the service that manages subscriptions for us, receives a random customer code, your account ID once you are signed in, the purchase receipt, and your device’s country and app version, so that the app and our server know whether Plus is on. We keep only whether you have Plus, which plan, and until when.

What we do not do

No advertising, no tracking across apps or websites, no selling or renting of data. This version of the app contains no analytics. If that ever changes, we will update this policy first.

We do not look at your memories in the normal running of the service. We look at content only when it is reported to us, when you ask us for help with it, or when the law requires it.

How long we keep data

Memories, photos, trail and profile: for as long as your account exists, or until you delete them.

A memory you delete: removed from your phone at once. On the next sync its content is erased on the server and its photos are removed from storage. An empty record stays, so your other devices know it was deleted.

Comments: until you or the memory’s owner delete them. A deleted comment is hidden at once, and its record is erased when the account is deleted.

Entries in your Activity list: while your account exists. Entries about things you can no longer see are not shown.

Push tokens: until you sign out, the token stops working, someone else signs in on that phone, or you delete your account.

Your Plus status: while your account exists; it goes with the account.

Reports: as long as needed to handle them and to deal with repeated abuse. A report stays after either account is deleted, but is then no longer linked to that account.

Our providers keep short-lived technical logs and backups, which expire after a short period.

Deleting your data

A memory you delete is deleted for good. There is no trash.

Settings → Account → Delete account and data deletes, straight away, from our servers and from this phone: your account, all your memories, photos and trail, your profile and picture, your friendships, the comments and hearts you made, the photos you added to friends’ memories, your tags in friends’ memories, your activity entries and your push tokens. If you signed in with Apple, Yulovo’s access to your Apple ID is revoked too.

Our provider’s backups may keep a copy for a short period until they are replaced.

Signing out removes your account’s data from this phone. It stays in your account and comes back when you sign in again.

Your rights

Under Vietnamese law you have the right to be informed, to give or refuse consent, to access your data and get a copy, to correct it, to have it deleted, to restrict its processing, to object, to withdraw consent, to complain to the authorities and to claim compensation. If you live elsewhere, for example in the EU or the UK, you have similar rights under your local law, including data portability.

In the app: edit a memory to correct it. Write to us (below) for a copy of all your data and photos. Delete as described above. Withdraw consent by turning off Explore mode or notifications, changing permissions in iOS Settings, signing out or deleting your account. Withdrawing consent does not affect what was processed before.

For anything else, write to timuregamov2000@gmail.com. We may ask you to confirm the request comes from your account. We reply within 30 days at most, and sooner where the law requires a shorter time. In Vietnam you can also complain to the Ministry of Public Security’s Department of Cybersecurity and High-Tech Crime Prevention (A05).

Data outside your country

To run the app, data is handled in Singapore (Supabase), the USA (Expo, Apple, Google), Germany (komoot Photon) and wherever OpenFreeMap’s tile servers are. We use only these providers and only for the purposes above. By using these features, you agree to this transfer.

Security

We use encrypted connections, server access rules and private file storage, and we keep no passwords. No system is perfect. If a breach affects your data, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

Children

Yulovo is not intended for children under 13. If you are under 16, you need the consent of a parent or guardian to use the app and to create an account. If we learn that a child under 13 has an account, or a child under 16 without that consent, we will delete it. Parents can contact us at any time.

Changes to this policy

We update this policy when the app changes how it handles data. The effective date is at the top. For important changes we will also tell you in the app before they apply.
